How to write a date in Roman numerals

A date has three parts — day, month, and year — and each is written as its own Roman numeral, separated by a small mark. For example, March 14, 2025 becomes III · XIV · MMXXV in month-day-year order. This is the format most people choose for a tattoo, anniversary band, or engraved gift, because it turns a personal date into something that reads like a classic monument inscription.

Pick the order that matches your meaning

  • Month · Day · Year — the common US layout (III · XIV · MMXXV).
  • Day · Month · Year — the layout used across most of the world (XIV · III · MMXXV).
  • Year · Month · Day — a clean, formal look some prefer for jewelry.

Whichever you pick, keep it consistent — and if it's going on your body forever, double-check the order against how you'd naturally read the date out loud.

Roman numeral basics

I = 1V = 5X = 10L = 50
C = 100D = 500M = 1000IV = 4, IX = 9

Numbers are built largest-first, and a smaller symbol before a larger one means subtract: IV is 4, IX is 9, XL is 40. There is no zero in Roman numerals, which is why a day like the 4th is IV, never IIII.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • IIII instead of IV. The correct short form for 4 is IV (though clock faces sometimes use IIII stylistically).
  • Mixing up day and month. The 3rd and the 12th look very different in numerals — confirm the order before you commit.
  • The year. 2025 is MMXXV (1000 + 1000 + 10 + 10 + 5). This tool does the arithmetic for you so it's always right.
